### Exothermic Reactions
**Exothermic reactions** release energy in the form of heat to the surroundings. This means that the energy of the products is lower than the energy of the reactants. The heat released is a result of the bonds formed in the products being stronger than the bonds broken in the reactants.

**Criteria for Exothermic Reactions:**
- **Heat Release:** The surroundings feel warmer.
- **Thermometer Reading:** The temperature of the surroundings increases.
- **Energy Profile:** The energy level of the products is lower than that of the reactants.

**Example: Combustion of Methane**
The combustion of methane (CH₄) is an exothermic reaction:
\[ \text{CH}_4(g) + 2\text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{energy} \]
In this reaction, methane reacts with oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and water, releasing a significant amount of heat.

### Endothermic Reactions
**Endothermic reactions** absorb energy from the surroundings. This means that the energy of the products is higher than the energy of the reactants. The heat absorbed is a result of the bonds formed in the products requiring more energy than the bonds broken in the reactants.

**Criteria for Endothermic Reactions:**
- **Heat Absorption:** The surroundings feel cooler.
- **Thermometer Reading:** The temperature of the surroundings decreases.
- **Energy Profile:** The energy level of the products is higher than that of the reactants.

**Example: Photosynthesis**
Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction where plants absorb sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen:
\[ 6\text{CO}_2(g) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{light energy} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(aq) + 6\text{O}_2(g) \]
In this process, plants absorb light energy from the sun to drive the reaction.

Yes, a process can involve both exothermic and endothermic reactions. Such processes are often referred to as **coupled reactions** or **multi-step reactions**. In these processes, one reaction releases energy (exothermic), and another absorbs energy (endothermic).

**Example: Thermite Reaction**
Thermite reactions are a classic example where a process involves both exothermic and endothermic steps. Thermite is a mixture of aluminum powder and iron oxide. When ignited, it undergoes a highly exothermic reaction that produces molten iron and aluminum oxide:
\[ 2\text{Al}(s) + \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}(l) + \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{heat} \]
This reaction is highly exothermic, releasing a lot of heat. However, the initial mixing and preparation of the thermite mixture might involve some endothermic processes due to the physical and chemical preparation steps.

- **Endothermic Reactions:** Absorb heat; products have higher energy than reactants.
- **Real-World Example:** Combustion of methane (exothermic) and photosynthesis (endothermic).
- **Coupled Reactions:** Processes like thermite reaction involve both exothermic and endothermic steps.
