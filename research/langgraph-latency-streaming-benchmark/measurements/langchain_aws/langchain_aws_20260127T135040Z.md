### Exothermic Reactions
**Definition:** Exothermic reactions are chemical reactions that release energy in the form of heat to their surroundings. This means that the energy of the products is lower than the energy of the reactants.

**Criteria:**
- **Heat Release:** The primary criterion is the release of heat.
- **Temperature Increase:** The surroundings typically become warmer.
- **Enthalpy Change:** The change in enthalpy (\( \Delta H \)) is negative (\( \Delta H < 0 \)).

**Example:** Combustion of methane (\( CH_4 \)) in oxygen (\( O_2 \)) to form carbon dioxide (\( CO_2 \)) and water (\( H_2O \)).

### Endothermic Reactions
**Definition:** Endothermic reactions absorb energy in the form of heat from their surroundings. This means that the energy of the products is higher than the energy of the reactants.

**Criteria:**
- **Heat Absorption:** The primary criterion is the absorption of heat.
- **Temperature Decrease:** The surroundings typically become cooler.
- **Enthalpy Change:** The change in enthalpy (\( \Delta H \)) is positive (\( \Delta H > 0 \)).

**Example:** The reaction between calcium carbonate (\( CaCO_3 \)) and hydrochloric acid (\( HCl \)) to form calcium chloride (\( CaCl_2 \)), water (\( H_2O \)), and carbon dioxide (\( CO_2 \)).
\[ CaCO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow CaCl_2 + H_2O + CO_2 + \text{heat (absorbed)} \]

\[ C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{energy} \]

In this example, the initial step is endothermic (absorbing energy), while the subsequent step is exothermic (releasing energy).

### Summary
